[ 01 / 06 ]Overview

About CARS24

CARS24 sells cars through a network of dealer partners. Alongside a vehicle, a dealer can sell value-added services such as extended warranty, roadside assistance, buyer protection and maintenance packages.

Overview

Warranty was bought one car at a time, after a sale, through a Relationship Manager. I redesigned the experience so dealers could purchase plans in volume, hold them as stock and activate each unit against a car later. The two halves had to remain legible as one transaction.

My role

I was the product designer on the project in 2025. I owned product thinking, the dealer journey, interaction design, final UI and engineering handover.

[ 02 / 06 ]Context

The dealer was buying one policy at a time. The business ran in batches.

A dealer might know roughly how many cars would be sold that month, but could not buy warranty capacity ahead of time. Each car reopened a long process across the dealer, a Relationship Manager and Warranty Operations.

Three groups had to make one sale work.

Dealer

Sold the car, requested cover, checked what was available and needed the policy active before delivery.

Relationship Manager

Bridged the dealer and internal teams, including balance lookups, inspection dependencies and status questions.

Warranty Ops

Checked eligibility, processed requests and completed the operational steps needed for issuance.

The design problem was not how to make the same 14 steps look shorter. It was how to stop repeating the purchase for every car.

[ 03 / 06 ]Problem

Four points of friction sat between a dealer and an active warranty.

Repetition

Buying after every sale meant the commercial decision had to be made again for each vehicle.

Invisible stock

Dealers tracked what they believed they owned in spreadsheets, not in the product where they would use it.

Lookups

Relationship Managers answered questions about balance and status that the interface could have answered directly.

Timing

Eligibility, inspection and customer registration still had to happen before a car was delivered. A bulk order alone could not solve activation.

What informed the model

Dealer and internal-team inputs pointed in the same direction: dealers planned for monthly volume, stock lived in spreadsheets, RM calls often asked for balances, and activation had to finish before delivery.

01 / Scope

Warranty first

The commerce model could later serve other value-added services, but warranty had to work end to end first.

02 / Coverage

Underwriter rules stay intact

Bulk stock could not promise cover for a car that failed plan eligibility.

03 / Inspection

Inspection is the source of truth

The interface had to read the inspection record, not replace it with dealer-entered eligibility.

[ 04 / 06 ]Approach

Treat the plan as stock, then spend a unit on a car.

The product model separates the commercial commitment from fulfilment. The journey starts when warranty is discovered in Miles. The bulk-sale screens connect a selected dealership, plan, quantity and payment handoff. Purchased units then become visible in the dealer app, where an eligible unit is assigned to a car and activated after inspection and customer details are complete.

01 / BuyPurchase plans

Choose coverage, tenure and quantity; confirm the order.

02 / HoldSee inventory

Available, added to car and activated counts stay visible.

03 / AssignMatch a car

Start from a car or from a purchased plan, with eligibility checked.

04 / ActivateFinish issuance

Inspection, car verification and customer details complete the policy.

Read the flow as steps
  1. Miles app: Open Warranty from Service add-ons and select the dealership.
  2. Bulk sale: Choose the plan and quantity, review the order and share the payment link. Paid units appear in Purchased Plans.
  3. Dealer app: Start from Purchased Plans or from a car. Choose one unit and one eligible car.
  4. Issuance: Check the inspection record, open the redemption cart and review the ₹0 bill, then complete the activation checklist and issue the policy.
  5. Exceptions: Inspection failure shows a removal or reinspection path. Activate Later moves the car to Pending Activation, where the dealer can resume.

The rules behind the screens

01

Separate buying from activation without losing the link between them.

02

Keep quantity, total, savings and unit price together.

03

Show what is available, assigned and still waiting.

The critical distinctionBuying five warranties does not mean five cars are covered. Each unit becomes a policy only after it is assigned to an eligible car and activation is completed.

Bulk Sales – Designs

Make the volume decision and payment handoff legible.

Coverage, tenure, quantity and effective unit price belong together. The bulk-sale board also shows a payment link shared with the dealership through the dealer app, WhatsApp and SMS, so the commercial step has a visible handoff rather than disappearing into the next flow.

A plan card makes the threshold visible

  • Problem identified

    A dealer comparing plans could not evaluate a discount without knowing how many units were required.

  • What I did

    The plan comparison places the quantity threshold with coverage and tenure. The dealer can assess the offer before entering the cart.

Inventory answers the next question

  • Problem identified

    After the order, the dealer still needs to know what is left and what has happened to each unit. A single purchased count is not enough.

  • What I did

    The Purchased Plans view separates available units from those added to cars and those activated. Expiry and count-drop rules are explained where the balance is shown.

Two entry points meet at the same redemption

  • Problem identified

    Some dealers start with a sold car. Others start from the warranty units they have already bought.

  • What I did

    The car detail page offers Redeem Purchased Plans, while inventory offers an eligible-car route. Both lead to selecting a unit for a particular car.

A zero-rupee bill explains itself

  • Problem identified

    A redeemed unit has already been paid for. Showing ₹0 without context could look like a pricing error.

  • What I did

    The redemption cart shows zero cost and says the plan was already purchased. The bill closes the loop between the earlier bulk order and this car.

Activation is a checklist, not one long form

  • Problem identified

    Inspection, vehicle verification and customer details depend on one another. A half-finished form makes it hard to see what remains.

  • What I did

    Three named steps show the work in order. Verification reads the inspection record and asks only for the odometer; customer details finish registration.

The exception states are part of the flow

A blocked car names the inspection issue and offers removal so the unit is not stranded. “Activate Later” leaves the car in a Pending Activation queue, where the dealer can return to finish the policy.

What I would measure next

  • Stock utilisation. How many purchased units are assigned, activated, left unused or expired? The inventory model is only useful if stock turns into cover.
  • Activation time and handoffs. Track the time from assigning a unit to an active policy, and count the status lookups and operations tickets that still need a person.
