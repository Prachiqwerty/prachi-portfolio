Overview

Warranty became inventory a dealer could plan around.

A dealership does not operate one vehicle at a time. It manages a changing stock of cars, multiple customer conversations, and recurring value-added-service demand. The warranty experience needed to reflect that operating model without making price, eligibility, or coverage harder to understand.

The redesigned flow lets dealers review compatible plans, purchase several units together, see the commercial benefit of quantity, and keep the order ready for later activation.

Core product shift

Separate the moment a dealer purchases a plan from the moment a warranty is assigned to a car.

The Problem

A dealer buying for many cars still moved through a one-car mental model.

Warranty products contain real complexity—coverage, vehicle eligibility, tenure, pricing, and activation rules. Repeating the same decision for each car added operational effort and made it difficult to turn volume into a clear commercial advantage.

01

Repeated purchase effort

Buying plan by plan forces a high-volume dealer to repeat selection and checkout instead of creating one useful stock of warranties.

02

Price without scale

A unit price is not enough for a bulk decision. Dealers need to understand quantity, total cost, savings, and effective unit price together.

03

Activation too early

Tying purchase to one known vehicle prevents the dealer from buying ahead and assigning plans as inventory moves.

Product Reframe

Treat warranty plans as dealer inventory—not a checkout attached to one car.

The key decision was structural rather than visual. Purchase and activation became two moments in the same service lifecycle. Dealers could commit when pricing and demand made sense, then consume each plan when the matching car was ready.

One-car model

Select car → choose plan → pay → activate

Every warranty begins with a vehicle and repeats the complete purchase journey.

→
Dealer-inventory model

Choose plan → set quantity → pay → activate per car later

One order creates a usable stock of plans without hiding the rules that make each plan valid.

Design Principles

Four principles kept a bulk transaction clear and controllable.

01Separate purchase from activation.

Let dealers secure inventory without forcing them to identify the final vehicle at checkout.

02Make unit economics obvious.

Keep quantity, total price, effective unit price, original price, and discount visually connected.

03Preserve eligibility confidence.

Engine-capacity range, tenure, coverage, and applicability stay visible while the dealer makes a commercial decision.

04Keep every bulk action reversible.

Selection confirms before it changes the order; the added state and persistent cart make review and correction easy.

Solution Flow

One focused sequence moves from comparison to a reviewable bulk order.

The interface reveals complexity at the moment it becomes useful. Dealers begin with the plan, choose quantity in a focused layer, see the resulting economics, and return to the catalogue with a persistent route to the cart.

01 / Compare

Start with coverage and fit.

Budget and premium plans form the primary choice. Each product keeps its engine-capacity range, tenure, price, and coverage route visible before quantity enters the decision.

  • Plan families remain easy to switch.
  • Coverage details are available without leaving the purchase context.
02 / Select quantity

Isolate the one decision that changes the order.

A bottom sheet holds the dealer in context while making quantity the only active choice. Confirmation remains unavailable until a quantity is selected.

  • Large numeric targets make volume quick to scan.
  • A close action makes the step safe to abandon.
03 / Understand value

Translate quantity into unit economics.

Once five units are selected, the interface connects total cost, effective per-unit price, original value, and discount. The dealer can evaluate the order before committing it to the cart.

  • Selected quantity receives a clear active state.
  • The enabled confirmation action signals a complete choice.
04 / Review order

Return to the catalogue without losing purchase state.

The selected plan becomes an editable added state. A persistent summary shows service count and order value, keeping the cart available while the dealer continues comparing plans.

  • Inline quantity controls support quick correction.
  • The cart action remains anchored to the bottom of the screen.

Outcome

The workflow supported warranty commerce at dealership scale.

The released dealer experience supported more than 500 warranty sales in a month. Beyond throughput, the design established a reusable commerce model: select a service in quantity, understand its unit economics, purchase it as inventory, and activate each unit when the operational need arrives.

500+warranties supported in a month through the dealer platform.
Design outcome

A scalable purchase model for value-added services.

The product no longer assumes that every warranty purchase begins and ends with one car. This creates a clearer foundation for dealer inventory, repeat purchase, and later activation.

Measurement boundary

The available metric establishes live operational volume. Without a pre-launch baseline or controlled conversion data, it should not be read as incremental lift caused by the redesign alone.

Learnings

Four principles from designing commerce for dealer operations.

01Product structure should follow the operating model.

A one-customer checkout creates friction when the actual user manages inventory and repeated transactions.

02Separate commercial commitment from fulfilment.

Purchase and activation can happen at different times while remaining part of one understandable lifecycle.

03Bulk pricing needs more clarity, not more decoration.

Quantity, total, unit price, original value, and savings must remain mathematically and visually connected.

04State visibility builds confidence.

Selected, added, editable, and ready-to-review states help dealers trust a higher-value order before payment.
